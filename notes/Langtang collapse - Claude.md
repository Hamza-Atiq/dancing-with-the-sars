# Langtang Lirung collapse, 26 Aug 2026 — research record

Started 14 Nov 2026. Event facts are from ICIMOD's compilation page and the Wikipedia article "2026 Nepal–Tibet floods" (first 100,000 characters only; the rest unread). Both are secondary sources. NISAR facts are from NASA CMR queries.

## 1. What happened

| Item | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 26 Aug 2026, 08:37 Nepal time (02:52 UTC), from the seismic signal | both |
| Source | North face of Langtang Lirung (7,227 m), Langtang National Park, Rasuwa district | both |
| Location | About 28.3 N, 85.5 E; about 15 km east of Rasuwagadhi | Wikipedia |
| Failure zone elevation | 5,000–5,500 m (ICIMOD); 5,200–5,400 m (Wikipedia) | both |
| Drop | About 1,200 m into the Lhende Khola, a border stream | both |
| Block size | 0.2 km², about 600 m wide (D. Shugar) | Wikipedia |
| Volume | 197–492 million m³ | Wikipedia |
| Seismic size | Ms 5.2 (USGS), Mw 5.7 (GFZ), landslide-type signature; second smaller collapse about 3 h later (Ms 4.2) | both |
| Mechanism | Ice-rock avalanche that became a debris flow. Not a glacial lake outburst at the source | both |
| Speed | About 52 m/s (190 km/h) over the first 22 km; run-up about 400 m on valley walls | both |
| Runout | About 100 km down the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli | both |
| Rain | Essentially none | ICIMOD |

### Timeline on the day (Nepal time)
- 08:37 collapse. 08:40 flood reported at Rasuwagadhi; a camera at Gyirong Port shows the crossing destroyed about 7 minutes after the seismic signal.
- 09:00–09:05 hydrologists informed. 09:15 SMS alerts sent to about 679,000 people.
- 09:20 reported arrival at Betrawati, 56 km downstream. After 11:00 Malekhu.
- Tribhuvan Trishuli school evacuated about 900 children ten minutes before the flood arrived.

### Dam and lakes
- Contested: an IRDR Young Scientists analysis (Adhikari et al.) reads imagery from 25 and 26 Aug as showing failure already on the afternoon of 25 Aug, with debris blocking the river for 18–19 hours before a burst. USGS and ICIMOD treat 08:37 as the collapse itself. The Rasuwagadhi gauge showed no rise before it was destroyed.
- Two barrier lakes formed afterwards. Lake 1, about 2 million m³ near the Chhochen Khola – Purepu Tsangpo confluence: breach warning 27 Aug, slight overflow 28 Aug, largely drained 30 Aug (the Trishuli at Galchhi rose about 3 m again that day). Lake 2 is at high elevation near the collapse site.

### Impact (figures conflict between sources and dates)
- Nepal, 5 Oct 2026: at least 1,455 dead, 9,287 injured, 5,285 missing. Tibet, 16 Sep: 43 dead, 519 missing. Earlier headlines range from 95 to 390 dead.
- Gyirong Port / Rasuwagadhi crossing destroyed. About 7,570 houses destroyed. 12 hydropower projects damaged; about 430.7 MW (about 10.6 % of Nepal's hydro and solar) disrupted; workers trapped in tunnels at Upper Trishuli 1 and 3A.

### Why nobody was warned
- The source was above 5,500 m with no instruments; the signal looked like an earthquake; the blockage was upstream of Nepal's gauges; monsoon cloud hid the source from optical satellites; warning systems were tuned to rain and lake outbursts.

## 2. Reported precursors (the research opening)
- Satellite radar showed the slope moved up to 30 cm in the 3–4 weeks before failure (analysis attributed to Q. Brencher). ICIMOD.
- From 8 Jan to 18 Aug 2026 the glacier north of Langtang Lirung moved about 10 mm per month, accelerating in the weeks before collapse. Wikipedia. The 8 Jan / 18 Aug dates do not match NISAR's pass dates on track 098, so this was probably another radar satellite.
- Summer temperatures about 1.5–2 °C above the long-term record; 15–25 m water-equivalent glacier thinning since 1957; fractured gneiss and schist with thawing permafrost.
- Earlier events at the same place: the 2015 earthquake avalanche that buried Langtang village (about 350 dead); the 8 July 2025 Purepu lake outburst that destroyed Friendship Bridge.

## 3. NISAR data at the site (point 85.52 E, 28.25 N)
- Track 098 A 016: 27 GCOV dates, 28 Nov 2025 – 18 Oct 2026, every 12 days except the instrument gap (26 Jul → 19 Aug). 10 m dual-pol.
- Track 048 D 074: 9 dates, 29 Jun – 15 Oct. Track 149 D 074 covers the valley downstream.
- Last passes before the collapse: 16 Aug (track 048) and 19 Aug (track 098). First after: 28 Aug and 31 Aug.
- The instrument gap (27 Jul – 10 Aug) falls inside the reported 3–4 week precursor window, so the pair 26 Jul → 19 Aug spans 24 days.
- Urgent Response processing starts 28 Aug: 32 granules.
- Validated collections start 21 Oct 2026 and do not contain the event.

## 4. Tests under way
- Full-resolution backscatter (10 m, HH and HV) for 14 Jul, 26 Jul, 19 Aug, 31 Aug, 12 Sep, 24 Sep on track 098: change map across the event, compared with change across a quiet interval.
- GUNW phase and GOFF offsets for pairs starting 8 Jun, 20 Jun, 2 Jul, 14 Jul, 26 Jul, 19 Aug: is the reported pre-failure movement visible?

## 5. Limits that shape the project idea
- NISAR revisits one track every 12 days. With three tracks the site is seen roughly every 4 days at best. A check "every 24 hours" is not possible from NISAR alone.
- Standard products appear days after acquisition; Urgent Response is faster but is switched on after an event.
- The flood reached the border crossing within minutes, so warning after the collapse was never possible from orbit. The usable window is the weeks of slope movement before it.
- Steep terrain causes radar layover and shadow; the north face is seen differently by ascending and descending passes.
